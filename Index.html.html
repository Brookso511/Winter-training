The mission

Arrive at the start of track season faster, stronger, healthier, and ready to handle race-specific work.

Nov 14–27

Reset & rebuild

No running for two weeks after cross-country. Light, controlled lifting can begin November 14.

18–25

December mileage

Gradually build weekly mileage toward this range. Start at the low end and earn each increase.

25–35

January–February

Build toward 25–30 miles, with an occasional 35-mile week only when recovery and consistency support it.

Important: Your previous injuries matter. This plan intentionally excludes deadlifts and back squats. Keep all lifting pain-free, use a coach or qualified strength professional to check technique, and do not increase mileage and workout intensity at the same time. If pain changes your stride or persists, stop the session and get assessed.

Workout menu by phase

These are workout templates, not a requirement to complete every rep. Use the treadmill only if it is safe for sprinting; a track or flat straight is better for true maximum-velocity work.

Phase 1 · Return to running (Nov 28–Dec 13)

Monday: Easy run, then 4–6 × 8–10 second relaxed hill strides or flat strides at about 70–80%, full walk-back recovery. Skip strides if anything feels off.

Wednesday: Easy conversational run only, 20–35 minutes.

Friday: Easy run with 4 × 15 seconds relaxed pickups, not a hard interval session.

Keep all running comfortable. No all-out 20–30m sprints yet.

Phase 2 · Build rhythm (Dec 14–Jan 3)

Monday — acceleration: 4–6 × 20m accelerations at 85–90%, then 3–4 × 60m fast but relaxed at 85–90%; 2–4 minutes recovery. Start with fewer reps.

Wednesday — aerobic: 20–35 minutes easy, or 6–8 × 1 minute steady / 1 minute easy. Keep it controlled.

Friday — introductory endurance: 6 × 200m at controlled, even effort with 200m walk/jog recovery. Not an all-out test. Reduce to 4 reps if form fades.

Phase 3 · Speed + strength (January)

Monday — fast twitch: 2 sets of 3 × 30m accelerations (90–95%), 2–3 minutes between reps and 5 minutes between sets; finish with 3 × flying 20m at fast, relaxed speed if experienced and pain-free.

Wednesday — aerobic strength: 2–3 × 1200m at controlled threshold effort with 2 minutes easy jog recovery, or 20–30 minutes continuous easy running in a lighter week. Keep this comfortably hard, not race pace.

Friday — speed endurance: 5–6 × 300m at controlled 800m/1500m effort, 2–3 minutes recovery. Progress toward 4–5 × 400m only when the 300s are smooth.

Phase 4 · Track preparation (February–March)

Monday — speed: 4 × 30m accelerations + 4 × 60m fast relaxed, full recovery. Every rep should look sharp; stop before speed drops.

Wednesday — longer threshold reps: 2–3 × 1600m at controlled threshold effort with 2–3 minutes easy jog recovery, or 2 × 1200m in a lighter week. Keep the effort steady and comfortably hard, not an all-out test.

Friday — longer reps: Rotate 5 × 400m, 3–4 × 600m, and 2 sets of 3 × 200m. Keep reps controlled and consistent, with 2–4 minutes recovery (longer for 600s). Do not race every workout.

For every quality session, adjust pace to current fitness rather than chasing old PRs. If you cannot maintain mechanics, stop the fast work. Do not add extra reps just because you feel good that day.

Example of a challenging training week

Here is what a solid January training week could look like once you have rebuilt consistently and are handling 25–30 miles per week comfortably. These are genuinely demanding sessions, but the goal is quality—not exhausting yourself so badly that the next workout suffers.

Monday · Fast speed / fast-twitch

HIGH INTENSITY FULL RECOVERY

  • Warm-up: 10–15 min easy jog, dynamic mobility, sprint drills, then 3–4 progressive strides.
  • Acceleration: 4 × 30m fast accelerations, taking 2–3 min recovery.
  • Speed: 4 × 150m at about 90–95% of your current top speed, with 4–5 min walking recovery. Every rep should be fast and technically clean.
  • Cool-down: 10–15 min easy jog/walk, then gentle mobility.
  • Lift A afterward, but keep the lower-body work moderate if the sprinting was especially demanding.

This is a high-quality speed session, not a conditioning test. Stop the fast reps if you noticeably slow down, tighten up, or lose mechanics.

Wednesday · Controlled tempo / aerobic strength

MODERATE EASIER THAN MONDAY & FRIDAY

  • Warm-up: 10 min easy jog, mobility, and 2–3 relaxed strides.
  • Main set: 2 × 1200m at controlled threshold effort, with 2–3 min easy jog recovery. As fitness builds, progress to 3 × 1200m or 2 × 1600m; choose one option, not both.
  • Cool-down: 10 min easy jog.
  • Lift B: upper-body strength and trunk work. Keep it controlled.

This is a longer aerobic-strength session: you should be working and breathing harder, but still feel in control. Start with 2 × 1200m; progress only after you can keep both reps even and finish feeling like you could do a little more. This should not turn into an all-out interval session.

Friday · Speed endurance / longer reps

HIGH EFFORT CONTROLLED PACING

  • Warm-up: 12–15 min easy jog, dynamic mobility, drills, and 3–4 progressive strides.
  • Main set: 5 × 400m at a strong, even effort—roughly current 3K–5K effort to start—with 2:00–2:30 easy recovery. Aim for consistent splits rather than a fast first rep.
  • Progression option after several successful weeks: 4 × 500m or 3 × 600m at a similar controlled effort, with 2:30–3:00 recovery. Do not add these on top of the 5 × 400m.
  • Cool-down: 10–15 min easy jog/walk.
  • Lift C afterward only if your legs still feel coordinated; reduce or skip lower-body sets when needed.

The Friday session should challenge your ability to sustain effort. It should not be an all-out race simulation every week.

How to make the plan harder safely: First complete the prescribed reps at consistent pace and good form. Then progress just one variable—one extra rep, slightly quicker pace, or slightly shorter recovery—not all three at once. Keep Wednesday easier than the two key days. In December, use the earlier return-to-running sessions rather than jumping straight into this January example.

Strength training · 3 days per week

Focus on powerful, technically clean movement, single-leg strength, hamstrings, calves, trunk control, and upper-body maintenance. No deadlifts or back squats.

Lift A · Monday

Lower-body power + upper push/pull · 35–50 min

  • Box jumps: 3 × 3 (low box, crisp landings)
  • Bulgarian split squat: 3 × 6 each leg
  • Hip thrust or glute bridge: 3 × 8
  • Push-ups or bench press: 3 × 6–10
  • Chest-supported row: 3 × 8–10
  • Standing calf raise: 3 × 12

Lift B · Wednesday

Upper-body maintenance + trunk · 30–40 min

  • Pull-ups or lat pulldown: 3 × 6–10
  • DB incline press: 3 × 8
  • Seated cable row: 3 × 10
  • DB shoulder press: 2 × 8 (if comfortable)
  • Side plank: 3 × 30 sec each
  • Dead bug: 3 × 8 each side

Lift C · Friday

Posterior chain + single-leg stability · 30–45 min

  • Low pogo hops: 2 × 10 (only if pain-free)
  • Step-ups: 3 × 6 each leg
  • Seated or lying hamstring curl: 3 × 8–10
  • Single-leg glute bridge: 3 × 8 each
  • Calf raises: 3 × 12
  • Farmer carry: 3 × 30m
How hard? In the first two weeks, do 1–2 easy sets per movement, light loads, and leave 3–4 reps in reserve. Build toward the listed sets over 3–4 weeks. Most strength sets should finish with 2–3 good reps still possible. Avoid max lifts, training to failure, and painful ranges. If any listed exercise resembles a previous injury trigger, replace it with a pain-free alternative chosen with your coach or physio.

Warm-up, cool-down & recovery

Before every run

  1. 5–10 min easy jog (or brisk walk during the return phase).
  2. Dynamic mobility: leg swings, ankle rocks, walking lunges.
  3. Running drills: A-skips, marching, high knees, 2 × 20m each.
  4. On workout days: 3–4 progressive strides, building gradually.
  5. For sprint days: add 2–3 short build-ups before the first fast rep.

After every run

  1. 5–10 min easy jog or walk.
  2. Gentle calf, quad, hamstring, and hip-flexor stretches.
  3. Drink fluids and eat a meal/snack with carbohydrates and protein.
  4. Note soreness, energy, sleep, and how the workout felt.

Recovery rules

  • Keep easy runs genuinely conversational; no pace target is needed.
  • Take Sunday fully off. Make Saturday optional, not compulsory.
  • Sleep consistently and eat enough to support training and growth.
  • Every 3–4 weeks, reduce mileage and workout volume by roughly 15–25% if fatigue is building.
  • Do not do hard sprinting on tired, sore, or cold muscles. Warm up longer in winter.

Training log

Use this quick weekly check-in to keep the plan honest. The best week is the one you can recover from and repeat.

Before the week

Set a realistic mileage range, choose the workout versions you can complete with good form, and note any soreness.

After each workout

Record distance, reps, effort (1–10), recovery, and any pain. Do not hide symptoms to hit a target.

End of the week

Ask: Did I recover? Did my mechanics stay good? If not, repeat or reduce the week instead of progressing.
